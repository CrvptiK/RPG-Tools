She halted, dumbfounded. “You’re not joking.”

“That’s impossible.”
“Yeah, that’s exactly- exactly what I thought when I first, uhm, encountered it?”
“How did you even notice it? I mean- No, no, forget it, nevermind. Something’s gotta be causing this, there- there has to be a logical explanation! We didn’t just come across Norton’s fucking dome, there’s gotta be- there’s a logical- there’s some reason for this!”
Aldric provided nothing but an awkward almost-shrug. Jannet scoffed. A moment of silence followed, during which she watched his every move. The younger scientist seemed even more on edge than was normal – his normal. Finally, it clicked.
“There’s a magnet in this thing, isn’t there?! Who put you up to this? Henry? Of course! And I fell for it, oh my god!”
“What? No, no I-”
“You’re a horrid liar, Al”, she leaned down to check under the table, “Or did ya use something else? C’mon, spill!”

Jannet laughed, shaking her head and turning away. “Ok, you got me. Wanna laugh about it over tea and coffee?”
“What? Jannet, no-” Her annoyed gaze shut him up immediately. He would just have to figure this out on his own.

Safe for the one assuming total chaos. Which was, to be clear, hardly a proper theory at all. His breathing grew faster, even as his mind tried everything in its power to provide some sort of comfort, some way to make sense of it. Absently he walked to the sink, letting slightly warm water run over his hand, which still felt searingly hot, gradually replaced by an uncomfortable tingle.
Time to make another note and run a new array of tests, he supposed. Something in the back of his mind meanwhile tried to warn him of pursuing the irregular – more so the insane.

### Chapter II — This Sinking Feeling

01/31/2025 Friday?

Aldric did not usually get lost. He could not even remember the last time it happened, if ever. So this was certainly unusual.
Worse, no matter the direction he turned, all he could see were illuminated street lanterns, every single one looking the exact same, as if someone repeated a copy-paste a hundred times over. 
Some tiny part of him tried to come up with a logical reason for this. He had simply wandered into an industrial area he had not been to before. But then again, there would have to be something – people, cars, buildings, even just a flickering lamp – anything. It had not even been half an hour and yet, the moon stood high in the sky. He could not have left the city in that time. No, this wasn’t it.
Maybe it was a dream? This was the single best explanation he could come up with. But then again, usually one did not recognize the state of dreaming. If this was a dream, surely he could tell somehow. Or maybe even influence it. His eyes landed on his hands, somebody had told him they always looked off in dreams. Yet, his hands looked perfectly normal.

Aldric picked up the pace of his walk, almost breaking into an actual sprint after a few minutes. This was nonsensical! Perception did not seem to be real here. It had to be a dream, it just-
He hit the street lamp – which had not been there half a second ago – full force, getting knocked back and off his feet. Glasses clattering to the floor, broke, his vision blurred beyond recognition, mainly by his tears. He wiped at his eyes. Instead, his hand came away doused in blood. That was probably why his face hurt so badly. His nose was definitely broken.
This was not a dream.

Sunday?

The moon had not moved an inch. But if his sense of time was anything to go by, it must have been hours, many hours. Certainly more than a day. His watch had stopped. It was not off otherwise, but no matter what he tried, he could not convince it to run, even though it worked via a pendulum mechanism, it did not even need a battery. He could not find his phone. And there was nothing to indicate proper time in this oddly vacant space.

He stumbled, feet catching on something on the ground. Looking down, his eyes fell upon train tracks. What? Where had they come from?
He looked around, eyes taking in his suddenly bright surroundings. The sun high above his head. 
To both sides of him, train tracks led to seemingly nowhere, going on and on forever. Classic blank sound barriers lined the tracks. No graffiti, no marks, not even individual signs lined the sides. Maybe Aldric should not be surprised by it. Yet he felt weird. Beyond the walls it seemed there was nothing either, no trees, no buildings one could see peeking over the metal. Or maybe he just could not tell from where he stood.

Now even more unsettled, he followed the tracks. At least he saw some natural light now, not just artificial. And yet, walking for what must have been hours did not feel any less draining. Continuing felt pointless after a while. The crack in his glasses started annoying him soon enough. But it was not as if he could do anything about this. So he simply continued walking.

Sunday? Maybe?

Aldric still had not found any way of telling the time. He had started counting at some point but once he got to five digits it became quite exhausting pretty fast. Instead he had tried to tell the time by distance traveled. Which as it turns out was a horrible idea when you had no way to recognize your exact position.

If he had to give it a guess he had wandered the tracks half a day, maybe more. He was frustrated by now. Curiously he had not felt any need to sleep, no thirst, hunger, nothing. This did not make telling time any easier. He shook his head, deciding it was time for a change. What did his mother use to say?

If forward and backward would not get him anywhere – quite literally, then maybe breaking out of it might help. Why did this remind him of the theories on dimension he had loved as a student? He was the stick figure on the two-dimensional pencil line, he supposed.
His gloved hands locked around the metal beams of an overhead line mast. He sure hoped he would not slip. Standing on the thin metal of the sound walls, he looked out over vast empty concrete. An endless landscape of nothing. Eyes widened, as they fell upon the single silhouette of a building in the far distance.

Monday? Presumably

Finally, he pushed against the doors of the train conduction building. But, when he stepped through the door frame, he stood on familiar ground. Ground level of one of the MIT laboratories. Computer Science building. Confused, he turned. The door behind him was locked now. Great. Aldric’s head throbbed. Only for a moment, before the sensation vanished again. He blinked, hand meeting his temple. When he finally shook off the stupor, the scientist took a look around. 

Fuck it, at least he knew this place. Which was why he grew even more irritated, not being able to find the way to his own office. Everything around him looked normal, it felt the same it always did. Yet, every path he took led him to a new room, a new hallway, a new lobby he could not remember ever being there. Frustration bubbled under his skin like liquid fire.
After arriving at yet another new set of stairs, he screamed. Until his voice gave out, he just screamed. Maybe someone would hear him. Maybe someone would help.

His voice broke. Vision blurred by a few stray tears, Aldric caught his breath, hands gripping the handrails so tightly it hurt. It took what must have been minutes for his heart to calm down enough to hear anything over the rush of blood in his ears. Not like there were any sounds to listen to really. 

Looking down, he could not even make out the bottom of the stairwell. Curious, seeing as he was still on ground level and this building did not have a sub-level. Normally, that was of course. Sighing, he gave up, following the stairs down. He did not expect to almost walk into still water. Dread crept up his spine, as he stared down at the murky liquid. A horrible death to die, he lamented, deciding not to dwell any longer and turning to walk back up the steps. 

Which was exactly when they fell out beneath him.

?

The water was freezing cold. The shock had caused him to try and breathe, resulting in saltwater worming into his lungs, filling into them, suffocating him. He broke the water’s surface, coughs wrecking him thoroughly, as his body fought to expel the liquid from his lungs. Finally, he breathed in air. But his plight did not end that easily. No matter where he looked, Aldric saw nothing but endless, unforgiving ocean.

He may have survived the initial cold shock, but soon enough his muscles wore out. He lost feeling in his fingers first. Soon arms and legs stiffened, as his body desperately forced all its heat to his core. 10 minutes. He knew that was all he had. And there was no way out of the water. Nothing to hold onto, as to not drown in the depths. Who was he kidding, even if he found something, he would not be able to hold onto it long anyway. Painfully aware of what this meant, but unable to just simply give up – I don’t want to die, I don’t want to die, please, I don’t want to die! – he continued floating for what could not have been more than a few painful minutes.

Gradually, not all at once, all feeling faded.


? Alive. Alive?

Waking up was not as painfully slow. One second to the next the threat of freezing and drowning, the weight of water, the darkness creeping in was gone, replaced with stale, musky air and dim, fluorescent light high above his head. Everything hurt. Trying to move resulted in nothing but agonized panting. Maybe he would just stay down for a few more minutes.

As he laid there, Aldric took in his surroundings. He was immediately reminded of the train tracks. A tunnel? A bunker? Something in between.
Either way, it appeared to be endless into both directions. Maybe he would just keep lying here for a few more minutes.

???

He could not sleep. No matter how hard he tried, how he turned and twisted, forced his eyes shut for what felt like forever, sleep never came. Tears did however. He was never going to get out of this place, was he?

He had gotten up, walking down the tunnel. If he could never leave, he could explore. Figure out the rules of this crazy- world? Dimension? Reality? His slightly manic laugh echoed back from the dull tunnel walls. Nothing around to see, safe for yellowed lights and stale gray walls. Maybe something would stick out. Anything.

????

Something did. Besides the flickering lights and the growing shadows, finally something changed. A single door. Metal. Unremarkable. Safe for the big white letters on a thick red stripe. Emergency Exit. He could cry. Elation was a fascinating feeling. So was surprise. Disbelief. Fury.

An error sounded. The number pad next to the door flickered to life. Enter Code. What kind of cruel joke was this? What safety exit would need a code? Questions upon questions tore at his mind, before he remembered. Of course. This place did not make any sense. He wracked his brain for any numbers he might have come across. In the end, he came up empty. The scientist had not walked past a single number. No odd observation one could read a number into. No nothing. He would have been the last person on earth to miss something like that.

Six digits. One million possible combinations. Only a handful he could think of to try first. None of which yielded any result. Safe for his immediate hatred for the error sound emerging. He looked through his pockets, finding his pen after a few moments. Might as well have a system while trying the – not impossible but – improbable. And so, he started. First all 6 digits identical. Then runs. When nothing yielded a result, he continued with his brute force systematic attempt. 0-0-0-0-0-0.

0-0-0-3-9-9

He was beginning to doubt his system. The human mind was not meant to continue something like this endlessly. This was what he used code for. Sadly, he did not have his laptop, had not seen even a single screen, did not even have his phone. And trying to pry open the little box taunting him from where it hung on the wall, incessant error sound ringing in his ears again, had resulted in nothing but a broken off finger nail and a slight jolt. He had considered it a warning and stopped attempting. He marked another row with a dot on his arm. Nothing again.

0-0-6-1-4-4

Taking breaks was not helping his focus anymore either. When he sat down, marking his last attempt on his arm in dots and lines – he had been forced to move to his other arm by now, he would never not have some notepaper with him in the future – if there was a future for him – his mind revolved only around the number pad and the next possible code. No amount of talking to himself, singing alone, trying to think of anything but this cursed number pad helped ease his mind. He got up again.

2-0-6-1-5-2

Aldric could not let his thoughts run free any longer. Whenever he did, he could think of nothing but how much he missed Mark. He wanted to go home. He missed his friends. He could not continue like this. He had run out of tears, and yet, the pain behind his eyes, throbbing inside his head continuously reminded him that he would cry if he could.

And then, finally, the error sound did not ring. The mechanism unlocked audibly. Aldric could hardly bring himself to celebrate, but somewhere deep inside of him, he felt immeasurable relief.

Not even one second passed by, before relief twisted into horror.

### Chapter III — Of Monsters and Men

08/13/2009?? No, wait.

Cold clawed at his insides, as his eyes took in the room in front of him. He had spent so many hours surrounded by those yellowing walls, trapped by them. Locked in. His childhood home still filled him with the same dread he had felt the last time he visited. Last time, when-
Desperately trying to calm his rapid breathing, hands cupping his mouth, looking around frantically, the room was spinning. When he turned there was no door. Turning again, the lock placed on the window when he was fourteen burnt into his field of vision, taunting him.

Sight. His homework on the small desk, the uniform folded neatly on his bed, books he had borrowed from the library stacked neatly against the wall, nothing to place them upon, ceiling lamp flickering, exposed wires he had attempted to fix back then, his tools inside the bag under his bed, most of them stolen.
Touch. His gloved hands covering his mouth, his glasses, still cracked, hair, just a fraction longer than how he remembered it, the carpeted ground beneath his shoes.
Sound. The staticky radio turned up downstairs, his father’s angry voice, dulled by the walls around him, his mother’s crying-
Aldric’s breath caught again. He had to help her!

He turned, immediately trying to force open the door. But even throwing himself against the wooden barrier resulted in nothing but a sharp pain rattling his shoulder. He hastily reached for the tools hidden beneath his bed, unscrewing the knob and making quick work of the mechanism.

It did not really matter how quickly he worked. Or how badly he tried. Or how desperately he wanted things to be different. Because in the end, he knew he could not save her. Back then, maybe he could have. Five years ago, begging her to leave that scum of a man. Shortly- just before she made that horrible, devastating choice. Made her last – her only – choice.
But now, it was too late. He was too late.

When he rushed out the door, he was no longer in that cursed house.

01/31/2025?? Friday???

He woke with a start, sitting upright momentarily. Sitting at the desk in his test lab. To his left, the yellow tape surrounding the not-space caught his eye. His laptop was still running calculations. The room was dim, only thin rays of moonlight and the faintest dots of stars could be spotted beyond the windows. He must have fallen asleep. It had been a dream after all.

Even though nothing seemed out of the ordinary – technically – some part of his subconscious was clawing at him, telling him something was off. Something was not right. He needed to leave. He needed to run.
Aldric checked his surroundings. Again. And again.
Classical Mechanics, Goldstein. He had given that book to Sophie at her request. He did not remember his student bringing it back yet. The file opened on his laptop did not quite follow his usual naming convention. His notepad was covered in calculations he did not remember writing down.

Getting up, maybe a little too fast, the room briefly swaying, the scientist left the room. He was met with yet another familiar sight, the darkened hallway, only partially illuminated by the single light above his head. The fluorescent light however barely managed to penetrate the dark. Aldric stared at it for several long moments, before shaking off the stupor and following the hallway. Casting a glance out the window at the end of the hall made him stop dead.
There was nothing beyond the windows. Initially he supposed it might be fog, or any other natural occurrence, but then again, mere minutes ago, he had been able to see the sky beyond the glass. Now it was only a deep void in front of him.
Closing his eyes and shaking his head, Aldric turned. He should know by now none of this would – could – make any sense. He would simply have to deal with it.
Walking back across the hallway, Aldric checked each and every door he came across. Locked. Every last one of them. But that hardly concerned him. He was too occupied with a different observation.
Humans, indeed most living things, have an innate sense for something else being around. People can practically feel, when they are being observed. That tingly feeling, like a feint breeze of air, making the hairs on your neck stand up. That odd sense of being watched. It hastened your heartbeat, lightened your steps, rushed your thoughts, until your world revolved around this one, single feeling.

I am not alone.

He reached the stairs. Once again, looking into both possible direction yielded the same results. Endless stairs. A faint taste of salt, the dread of not being able to breathe prompted him to head upstairs.
He hoped the feeling of being watched would wear off. Instead, it only seemed to get worse. Every step he took, every single breath of air passing his lips, he felt something behind him. Reaching out towards him.
The sound of something breaking, a crunching noise of something stepping on glass shards made his blood run cold. Without hesitation, he hastened his steps, breaking into a sprint up the stairs, away from the sound. 

It hardly took a second, before the noises behind him grew louder. Steps? Almost.
Deep down Aldric knew he did not want to know what was behind him. If whatever it was caught up with him, he knew it would not end well for him. And so, he ran.
He may not have been picked first in sports, he was horrible at most of them, way too talented in hurting himself, rather than succeed at whatever game was being played, but he was good at one thing. Running. And even better at running away.

Still, it did not take long, before his breaths grew shallow and rapid. Adrenaline was powerful, it had pushed him far past his limit. But the human body could only endure so much. When his foot caught on the last stair of a flight, his heart dropped, breath catching. He hit the ground hard.
Panicked, he pushed himself back up, his wrist protesting valiantly, as the sickening sensation of broken bones grinding against one another nearly caused him to black out. He could not stop though.

Grasping the handrails with his good hand, Aldric got to his feet.
It took him a few long seconds to realize what he was looking at, as his eyes locked on the window in front of him. It was the only one of its kind with a visible latch. And not only that. Beyond the glass, he saw light. Bright white and chasing away the darkness around him.

Previously distant noise still continued growing closer. With the faintest curse muttered under his breath – was that what he sounded like? – Aldric closed the distance, tearing open the window and, throwing all caution to the wind, climbed outside.

?

For a moment, Aldric fell. Then his back hit the ground, forcing all air from his lungs.
Once he had managed to get enough air back into his lungs, he looked around, almost frantic. The window was gone. In fact, there was no building at all. Instead he laid surrounded by an endless field of red flowers, every single one identical to the one before. He remained like that for a long moment, shutting his eyes, laying on the soft grass. His breath hitched briefly, as tears burned behind his eyes.

He could not say how long he remained there, too worn to force himself up.
Why even try? Why continue wandering this cursed place, if all it did was send him to experience the next inconceivable thing? He was tired. Not physically, not really. But mentally. He felt like he was going insane. The loneliness, the prospect of never making it home.

No. He could not think like that. He had to get up. He wanted – needed – to see make it home, he needed to see Mark, see his friends – he had plans for his life, plans he could not simply give up on. So he got up once again. And he started walking. He did not have the faintest idea where he was headed. But if he had learned a single thing about what this place entailed, he was looking for something irregular. Something different. Out of the ordinary. Something odd.

??

He had not noticed it immediately. Wandering for hours, that felt like days, or maybe it was days, Aldric quietly wondered how he had not noticed. How something like this had not caught his attention upon arriving. The sky. Periodically, not at all times, but often enough, the empty, blue sky would open up small distorted holes. The light around these holes fractured, as if overlaid with an effect reminiscent of chromatic aberration. Every time one such hole formed, the air would be filled with the faintest, unsettling smell of ozone and a sensation of static.

Deep down Aldric wished he could study these distortions. He wanted to know what this meant for reality. This was likely the most fascinating find he had come across up to this point. But there was nothing he could do to research these occurrences. And so he was stuck with continuing his path. Nothing to do but walk on.

???

He was watching the sky throw up several of its peculiar tears at the same time. A spectacle of color one could hardly comprehend. How he wished to somehow capture this moment in time. Once again, he wished for his phone. Not to reach out to anyone, but to hold onto this event. 

Was that so wrong? Yes. No.

When the sky calmed, so did Aldric’s mind. The smile on his lips slowly died. Why had he been smiling at this scientific impossibility in the first place? He frowned.

Turning to continue, he froze. His eyes fell upon something several feet ahead. Amidst the endless field of red petals stood a lonely blue flower. A single blue iris. This, surely, had to be unexpected enough. Aldric’s steps grew quicker, as he made his way towards the unique plant.
When he finally reached it, his eyes almost immediately found the tool laying next to it. A shovel. He frowned, looking around. Message received, he figured, picking it up. With one last glance towards the sky – maybe someone would believe his stories of this place, once they published their findings, people had to after all – he dug the sharp iron tool into the soft ground.

And just like that, he was somewhere else. Unable to breathe. Suffocating. Buried.

### Chapter IV — Sur/Reality

???

For a brief moment, Aldric lost all orientation. Breathing felt impossible. The taste of iron and earth coated his tongue, he could not see his own hands. His heart was beating out of his chest. Even though he knew panic was not helping the situation at all, he struggled to calm his breathing enough to finally think of a plan. He had figured out almost immediately what this was. He was in a coffin, beneath what was most likely several feet of earth. Trapped.

He took one more deep breath, before raising his knee to try and break the wood. Luckily, it gave way.
After several painful attempts, the coffin splintered and broke apart. He managed to tear the hole open a bit further, shuffling through the tumbling, dry earth that immediately fell to fill up the newly vacant space below. It took a while to force enough of the dirt and debris out of the way to even try and break through the earth above. He knew time was limited. Five hours, if he wanted to think very positive. Considering everything he guessed he would have an hour or two at most.

There was no way to know for sure how long it took him to reach fresh air. His hand breached the last barrier of earth above him, bloodied fingers flinching away, as they came in contact with the freezing air. It might have been a few minutes. To Aldric it had felt like hours.

Finally, he managed to pull himself up and out of the hole that, standing on the mound of dirt that had fallen, reached right up to his chin.
As he laid on the cool grass, tears in his eyes, blood dripping onto the green from his hands and knee, he could do nothing but take deep, still terrified, breaths.

How was he ever going to explain this to anyone?

That was, if he ever made it out. The more he thought about it, the less likely it seemed. He felt sick at the thought of being trapped forever. If this place – whatever it was – never let him go-
His breath caught in his throat, as he choked on a sob, forehead pressed to the ground. He just wanted to go home! He missed Mark, God, how much he missed him.

As he thought of him, lamenting the warmth of his hugs, the care in his words, the love in his eyes, more tears began to fall. He would do anything to go home.

What? 

Looking up slowly, upon the morning sun rising in the distance, chasing away the dark, Aldric’s eyes widened, taking in an all-too-familiar sight. Right in front of him, looking exactly like he remembered, was his home. Not the cursed walls he knew from his childhood, not the familiar but slightly off state he had found his workplace in. The apartment building looked… normal. In the best way imaginable.

In front of him laid his home, exactly like he remembered, a small package that was said to arrive the day he had been pulled from reality, waiting to be taken inside. Both his and Mark’s car parked next to the street. The bushes in front of the house, which Mark had cut down a little bit too harshly, struggling to hold on to dear life. That stupid doormat, Mark had insisted on buying, when he had seen Aldric grin dumbly at it at Walmart, greeted him, once he reached the door to their flat.

The scientist could not help but feel hope at the sight. Was it actually over? Was he home?
He shook off the thought. He had believed reality had reclaimed him one too many times. At this point, he was all but certain this was just another mirage. An illusion to build him up, right before breaking him down.
Unlocking the door felt awfully real. The turn of the key, the little click. When he entered, reality faded.

Normally Jelli would be by the door to greet him. And then Mark would round the corner, smiling at him. They would talk about their days, cook, eat, sit on the couch – either watching something together, or each doing their own thing. It was those moments that made life worth living. The small moments.
Now, no such moment awaited him. Jellicent was nowhere to be seen. And Mark did not greet him with a smile and offering a hug. He remained alone.

Seeing his home vacant of all things that made it his, was an odd feeling. Like someone was slowly scratching out everything of importance to him.

She knew, if Aldric left right now she would not see him again. She might not hear her son’s voice again. She may never hold him in her arms again. She did not have time to wait for Samuel to change his mind – if he ever would. She barely had time at all.

“That coward is no son of mine, Carolyn.”

He had thought his words could not touch him anymore. That it would not hurt to hear this. After all, Samuel was not his father. So why should he be his son?
And yet. It hurt. As a boy he had wished for nothing more, than for his father to approve of him, or to at least acknowledge him. As a teen, he had defied him in every way he could – thinking that, maybe, if he acted more like Blake, if he was bolder, if he tried harder, became smarter, maybe then, his father would look at him, just once, without disgust in his eyes. As a man, he had killed that belief – but maybe a small piece had remained.
He watched Samuel worriedly. Why would he let him go now? Had he not meant to kill him from the moment he had recognized him? Maybe Aldric would never know.

When his maman pulled him into a warm, loving hug, he remembered darkness was not all there was. And yet, it hurt so badly. The good times and the bad. Because he knew he had failed. His begetter would never let her go. She would never get the life she deserved.

Walking to and out of the door was the hardest thing he had ever been forced into doing.

The fresh air stung against the still-bleeding wounds on his face. After a moment, the door behind him opened. His mother slowly walked towards him.
“I am so sorry, darling”, she gathered his hands in hers. Blood glistened faintly in the starlight. “This won’t be forever. He’ll come around. I’ll see you someday.”
She pulled him into one final hug. Contrary to what she had claimed, this felt like goodbye.

He had not believed her words that day. And he was right not to.

He knew Samuel was watching them. Otherwise, maybe they could have run, still. He knew, deep down, his hopes were futile.
“Je t’aime, maman (I love you)”, he whispered through tears, while she held him.
“Je t’aime, ma lumière. Je serai toujours avec toi, où que tu ailles (I love you, my light. I will always be with you, wherever you go)”, she answered quietly, before pressing a light kiss to his temple.

He did not look back, as he walked away, knowing that if he did, he could not force himself to leave her.
